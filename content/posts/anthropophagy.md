## Anthropophagy and D&D

It can be argued that D&D culture is inherently anthropophagic to a degree, and always has been. It was always a collage of different cultural influences, even before release, or even written down. As time went on and it became oral tradition, the more true this became.

And true it was here in the global south, where cultural influences were not always the same, or had the same impact. Here in Brazil *sword and sorcery* wasn't that hot of a genre, the kind of science fantasy that Vance and his brethren wrote was nearly unheard of. Yet, the 80's cartoon was released before the game did, becoming our beloved *Caverna do Dragão*, Tolkien was still strong around general geekdom, and we had our stories and myths to fill in the voids. We could see a part of our [syncretic selves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Syncretic_Religions) in the pantheistic worlds presented to us, the tension of different faiths trying to work together at the same time weren't unknown, not at all. We to this day insert a tension in our fantasy faiths that still turns some gringo heads, to our amusement. *Anime* and *Tokusatsu* showed up here with force decades earlier, and they did it RAW, with no *adaptation*, as gloriously and explicitly Japanase as they could get, we had no use lying to our children that oniguiris were donuts. They knew other cultures and other peoples existed, They had no reason not to.
