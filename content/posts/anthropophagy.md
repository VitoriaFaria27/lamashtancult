The *Anthropophagic Manifesto*, from about a century ago, [proposes that Brazil's history of "cannibalizing" other cultures is its greatest strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto_Antrop%C3%B3fago). I would argue this is hardly an unique thing to Brazil, rather, a very common approach to culture from most of the global south, and even beyond.

It contrasts greatly with the usual protestant view of culture, which separates everything from what comes from *us*, and therefore, clean, holy and just, and what comes from the *other*, therefore dirty, unholy, sinful, that should never be touched by the righteous, else they become *tainted* by it.

We see the friction from these two different world views all the time in TTRPG discourse. When this happens, I'm not shy about my bias, I eat people, so this whole thing is going to be heavily biased towards an anthropophagic view point. I will go as far as claiming the protestant viewpoint is a tool of oppression, meant to divide minorities, keep them in their place and punish if they ever muster leaving it.

I could probably write a whole essay about how fucked up it was back when a bunch of dumb idiots wrote a Ghibli inspired supplement and half of the indie TTRPG space went _"culture is owned by blood"_. Don't get me wrong, when someone releases a lame, cash grabbing 5e supplement, you're all free to talk shit about it, but holy crap, _don't imply the global south needs to bow down and ask permission to ever mess with something Japanese inspired_, or worse, imply (or sometimes outright say) that it _can only be done if there's a Japanese person working on it_. No, just no, Japanese culture is not specially sacred, no culture is, draw Ghibli characters fucking each other for all I care, I'm pretty sure Miyazaki would have a nice laugh out of it.

Not to say that the concept of cultural appropriation is just complete bullshit, it does make sense in some very specific contexts, specially when you're taking something out of a gravely endangered culture that is under risk of simply being gone in a couple generations if not actively protected. Miyazaki ain't that, he is old, rich, and laughing at you, constantly, *as he should*.

But were not here to talk about that today, today we are talking D&D
